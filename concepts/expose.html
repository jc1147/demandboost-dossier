The story in brief

They paid for marketing.
They got a photocopier.

Ninety-seven chiropractic practices pay DemandBoost for marketing. This paper measured all of them, the same day, the same way: 97 homepages captured and read line by line, 95 clinics scanned point-by-point on a geo-grid for the one phrase that fills a schedule — “chiropractor near me” — and 97 websites speed-tested twice, as a phone visitor and as a desktop visitor.

The clinics did nothing wrong. They bought a service in good faith and sent the invoice every month. What the money bought is the story: 83 of the 97 “custom” websites run one of just 12 shared templates. 172 sentences repeat word-for-word across the book. 50 sites load a stylesheet literally named after the agency. The median client sits at position #8.9 on its own map, and the median client site takes 10.5 seconds to show its content on a phone.

When one clinic loses, that’s a market. When 82 of 95 lose the same phrase, the same way, on the same template — that’s a supplier. Only 13 clinics in this book hold a winning share of the phrase, and as the last section shows, they’re carried by an asset the agency didn’t build: their own patients’ reviews.
